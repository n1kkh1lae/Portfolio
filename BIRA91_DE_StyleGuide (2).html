Brand Story & Market Rationale
02 / 08
Context: Bira 91 is expanding into the German market with a tailored extension — Bira 91 DE. This guide governs all brand expressions for Deutschland: packaging, POS, and trade communications. Core Bira 91 identity is preserved; this document defines market-specific adaptations for regulatory compliance, cultural fit, and retail presence.
Origin
Born in Delhi.
Brewed for the world.
Bira 91 launched in 2015 with a single conviction: beer could be flavorful, playful, and genuinely Indian without being narrowly ethnic. "Bira" means brother in North Indian vernacular and echoes the Italian "birra." The 91 encodes India's international dialling code — national pride without nationalist weight.
The inverted B — Bira's most recognizable mark — was designed as an act of deliberate irreverence against category convention. The monkey mascot follows the same logic: the wild, playful side every drinker carries. Together these elements built one of India's fastest-growing beer brands, reaching 30% share in the premium segment without traditional advertising spend.
The German extension, Bira 91 DE, brings this philosophy to one of Europe's most discerning beer cultures. Bira 91 DE enters not to compete on German beer's terms, but to offer something categorically different: a wheat ale built for flavour, not ritual.

Why Germany, Why Now
Germany's craft segment has grown 18% YoY since 2021, driven by 25–40 urban consumers open to non-German styles. Indian food & culture penetration in Berlin, Munich, Hamburg, Frankfurt creates natural brand awareness infrastructure. Metro Deutschland's wholesale network provides immediate distribution access.

German Label Compliance (LMIV / EU 1169/2011): All mandatory declarations (ABV, allergens, volume, best-before) require minimum 1.2mm x-height. DM Sans at 7pt on uncoated stock meets this threshold. Playfair Display must not be used for regulatory text — high stroke contrast reduces legibility at small sizes. Umlaut characters (ä, ö, ü, ß) must be embedded in all PDF exports — do not use substitution glyphs. Verify all German copy with a native speaker before press approval.

Do / Don't
07 / 08
Brand Usage Rules
✓
Do
✓
Always use the inverted B as part of the wordmark. It is Bira's primary differentiator and must never be replaced with a standard "B".
✓
Apply the DEUTSCHLAND sub-tag on all DE-market packaging. Pantone-matched yellow or cream white only.
✓
Use wet-strength 80gsm paper for all bottle labels. German retail environments (refrigeration, condensation) require it.
✓
Embed all fonts and Pantone references in every press-ready PDF. Verify umlaut rendering (ä, ö, ü, ß) before sending to printer.
✓
Include the DPG Pfand recycling deposit mark on all 500ml bottle labels sold through German retail. Legal requirement.
✓
Use the monkey mascot consistently across all touchpoints. It is the emotional anchor of the brand.
✗
Don't
✗
Never use Deutschrot or Altgold on global Bira 91 materials. These colors are DE-market exclusive and will create brand inconsistency.
✗
Never place the logo on mid-tone backgrounds (grey, tan, olive). Contrast ratio drops below 3:1, failing legibility at shelf distance.
✗
Never stretch, rotate, or apply drop shadows to the wordmark. The inverted B already creates tension — additional distortion destroys legibility.
✗
Never use Playfair Display for regulatory or nutritional text. Its stroke contrast reduces legibility below 10pt. DM Sans mandatory for all small-print legal copy.
✗
Never approve RGB files for print. All artwork must be CMYK with embedded ICC profile (ISO Coated v2). RGB conversion causes unpredictable shifts on Pantone 116 C.
✗
Never translate the "91" numeral. It encodes India's country code — it is a brand constant, not a number to localize.
